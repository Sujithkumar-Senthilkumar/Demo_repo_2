## Description

The customer feedback portal is a lightweight system that lets customers submit and upvote product feature requests, view request statuses, and consult a basic public roadmap (MVP: submit, upvote, status, basic roadmap). Its primary users are customers who submit and vote on requests and the product/roadmap team that triages and prioritizes them; it exists to centralize feature requests and enable transparent prioritization to support the goal of prioritizing and committing the top 20 voted features to the roadmap within six months.

## Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Difficulty collecting and prioritizing feature requests
**Who is affected:** Customers who submit and upvote feature requests, and the product/roadmap team responsible for triage and roadmap commitments.

**Impact:** Feature requests are currently collected in a fragmented way and lack transparent prioritization, preventing the product team from reliably selecting and committing roadmap items. This directly jeopardizes the interview‑stated success metric to review, prioritize, and commit the top 20 voted features to the roadmap within six months of portal launch (interview: "Prioritize and commit top 20 voted features to roadmap").

**How this product solves it:** The feedback portal centralizes submission, upvoting, request status visibility, and a basic public roadmap (interview MVP: submit, upvote, status, basic roadmap), creating a single, transparent source of truth that lets the product team surface top‑voted items for review and formal roadmap commitment within the six‑month target.

## Key Features

- Accept customer feature submissions: the portal must let customers create and persist feature request records that include at least a title, description, submitter identity, and timestamp; submitted requests must be visible in the portal for triage and downstream actions.
- Enable community support signaling (upvote): the portal must allow users to register support for existing requests and surface vote totals on each request so relative demand is visible and quantifiable for prioritization.
- Provide visible request status tracking: the portal must display a lifecycle status for each request and retain a timestamped history of status changes so contributors can observe progress and decisions; status changes that represent roadmap commitments are controlled by the product/roadmap team.
- Publish a basic public roadmap linked to requests: the portal must present a simple, public roadmap view that lists planned and committed requests and links each roadmap entry back to its request record and vote totals, and where available, shows an indicated target window and an owner for transparency.
- Surface and prioritize top-voted items to support commitments: the portal must produce a ranked view or report of top‑voted requests to inform prioritization and enable the product/roadmap team to review community‑backed items; this output must be usable as input to a documented review workflow whose explicit business goal is to review and commit the top 20 community‑voted features within six months of portal launch.

## Success Criteria

- The program commits the top 20 customer‑voted features to the official product roadmap within six months of the portal launch; acceptance evidence: roadmap entries dated within the six‑month window that reference the portal item ID and include a recorded commit decision and timestamp (implements the interview goal to "prioritize and commit top 20 voted features to roadmap").
- The feedback portal is live in production with the MVP capabilities defined in KEY-001 (feature submission, upvoting, a visible status field per request, and a basic public roadmap); acceptance evidence: end‑to‑end verification that submissions persist request records (title, description, submitter identity, timestamp), authenticated upvotes increment visible vote counts, request statuses are displayed, and the public roadmap lists items with status.
- Engagement and progress metrics are collected and available for the six‑month measurement period to evaluate progress toward the top‑20 commitment: distinct submitters, distinct voters, total submissions, total upvotes, and a time series of status transitions; the specific engagement thresholds to qualify items for commitment will be defined and documented separately.
- Each roadmap commitment includes traceability to portal activity: the roadmap entry stores the portal item ID, the recorded vote count at time of commit, and a documented decision rationale or commit note; acceptance evidence: roadmap entries include these fields or attachments so reviewers can verify that top‑voted items informed the commitment.

## Scope

**In:** 
- The initial MVP capabilities defined in the interview and documented in KEY-001: customer submission of feature requests (persisting at least title, description, submitter identity, timestamp), voting/upvoting with visible vote totals, a request-status field for tracking each request, and a basic public roadmap that lists items and their status. 
- Mechanisms and acceptance criteria required to deliver and measure the MVP against the six‑month success outcome in SUCC-001 (prioritize and commit the top 20 voted features): 
 - Commit evidence: a committed item is evidenced by a roadmap entry dated within the six‑month window that identifies the feature and records the formal commit decision (as described in SUCC-001). 
 - Vote measurement window: only votes collected from portal launch through the six‑month selection cutoff will be used to identify the top 20 items; vote counts and timestamps must be exportable for verification. 
 - Basic vote integrity and manual review: the MVP will surface vote totals and provide simple anomaly flags (e.g., rapid spikes or repeated identical submissions) for internal review; a full automated anti‑abuse system is out of scope for the MVP. 
 - Internal triage and deduplication workflow: the product/roadmap team will perform internal moderation to consolidate duplicates and map submissions to canonical items prior to final vote aggregation; the public UI will not offer advanced merging or threaded discussion in the MVP. 
 - Minimal operational controls: internal roles/permissions to update request status, perform triage, and publish roadmap entries will be provided so status changes are controlled and auditable; enterprise‑grade access control or SSO integrations are not required for MVP. 
 - Basic reporting exports: simple exports/reports (e.g., ranked list by vote count, submission timestamps, and status rollups) necessary to select and document the top 20 will be provided. 
 - Governance artifacts: the product team will document the review and sign‑off workflow showing which top‑20 items were reviewed and formally committed, and Engineering will confirm capacity before scheduling (per the SUCC-001 commitment requirement). 
 - Compliance guardrail: the portal will capture and retain only the submission and vote metadata necessary to prioritize requests and will follow the organization’s existing privacy and retention policies; any broader data exports require Legal/Compliance approval.

**Out:** 
- Any capability beyond the interview‑defined MVP (submit, upvote, status, basic roadmap, and using votes to prioritize decisions) is excluded from the initial release. Reason: the interview explicitly limited the MVP to those items and the immediate six‑month success objective focuses on committing the top 20 voted features. Examples of excluded capabilities include (but are not limited to): full anti‑abuse automation, public or threaded discussions on submissions, advanced merging or collaborative editing in the public UI, enterprise SSO or advanced RBAC, deep analytics or dashboarding beyond the simple exports needed for selection, integrations that automatically create tickets in engineering systems, large‑scale import/export workflows, and broad localization or internationalization work for the portal.
